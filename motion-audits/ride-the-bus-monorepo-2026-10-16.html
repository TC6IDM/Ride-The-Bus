Ride The Bus: the round moves well, the rooms between don't

A pass over the loader, the hand-offs from loader to intro to board, the deal, the flip, the held last card, the win takeover and the panel arrivals. Weighted for a casino card game: polish first, delight on the rare moments, and restraint on the deal and the flip, which run every round.

Overall

Inside a round the motion is unusually well made: results wait for the card to turn, a slam finishes everything in flight, turbo scales every clock, and the held last card is one gesture on one clock. The problem is the space between rounds and screens. The intro leaves in a single frame, the loader's cards never land anywhere, and the deal spends its time on the part the eye cannot see.

02

Where the timings land

A game spends its time differently from a tool. A 1.24s takeover title is right for a moment a player sees once in seventy rounds. What matters is whether each leg gets the time its job needs.

The animations

  1. 1Intro → boardnone
  2. 2Loader fade-out320ms
  3. 3Deal: gather into the deck110ms
  4. 4Deal: whole, plus 300ms rest650ms
  5. 5Deal: stagger between cards45ms
  6. 6Panel arrival220ms
  7. 7Card flip (--flip-dur)500ms
  8. 8MODE sign roll340ms
  9. 9Takeover title (rare)1,240ms
Instant / Responsive · 0–300ms: picks, panels, presses.
Deliberate · 300–500ms: a card turning, a sign rolling, a screen handing over.
Sluggish · 500ms+: only where a game earns it: the takeover, the hold.
What's off

The deal's budget is upside down. The leg the eye has to follow (cards swept back to the deck) gets 110ms and reads as a teleport. The leg nobody can see (the 300ms rest inside the deck) gets the most. And the intro-to-board hand-off (1) has no transition at all.

The intro leaves in a single frame

WhatStartScreen is mounted by a bare {#if introPhase === 'start'} with no transition, and .ss-overlay has no exit. Continue sets introPhase = 'playing' and the four dealt panels, the logo and the dim are gone on the next paint.

Why it mattersIt is the first thing a new player presses. The intro arrives with a deal (ss-step-in) and leaves with a cut, so the most-seen transition in a session is half-built. The four panels teach the four cards, and they vanish rather than becoming them.

Recommended motionLet the four step panels collect down into the board's four card slots (shrink and travel, 420ms --ease-in-out, 60ms apart) while the overlay's dim lifts. The lesson becomes the table. Under reduced motion, a 150ms crossfade. This is item C's spine.

The deal spends its time where nobody can see it

WhatThe travel is flipDur × 1300 (650ms at 0.5s). The gather leg is 17% of that (110ms), the stagger is flipDur × 90 (45ms), then a 300ms DEAL_REST_MS spent invisible inside the deck, then 540ms back out. Last Stop's ticket rides the same curve.

Why it mattersAt 110ms a card crossing a third of the table reads as a teleport, and with 45ms between them the four cards move as one block. The owner reports "too fast". The time is there, but it sits on the rest, not the travel.

Recommended motionRe-balance, don't lengthen. Move the split, not the total: gather in about 220ms on the game's --ease-in-out (a card moving across the table), keep your 300ms rest, deal out over about 430ms ease-out, 80ms apart, and give the ticket its own beat after the last card. Card 1 still lands inside the reveal's 950ms hold-back. Turbo and the slam keep cutting it, and playDeal is re-timed against npm run audio.

CriticalJheyJakub

The loader deals four cards that never land

WhatThe loader deals four card backs into a row (loader-deal, 0.52s, 110ms apart), floats them, and then the whole loader fades for 320ms. At that moment the table's own four face-down cards and the intro are mounting underneath. The cards are not related to anything the player sees next.

Why it mattersIt is the first second of the game, and the owner's top priority. A deal that leads nowhere reads as a splash screen. The same four backs a moment later in different places read as two unrelated screens, which is item C.

Recommended motionKeep one world. The table is drawn from the first frame and the loader's cards are dealt onto the board's four slots (measured, as the deal already does) once ready. The progress lives on the table, not under it. 520ms ease-out, 90ms apart, the slot rects measured at rest.

The takeover's suit-mark burst reads as debris

WhatTen suit marks travel outward on straight lines over 1.5s (wc-mark-out, ease-out), low in contrast against the veil, and fade while still drifting.

Why it mattersA straight, slow, faint drift is how dust moves. Thrown things arc, spin and land. The burst should be the hand's thrown chips, the one beat that says "something left the hand".

Recommended motionFewer, bigger, harder. Six marks thrown on an arc (an x track on ease-out and a separate y track that rises then falls), turning as they go and gone by about 900ms, in full ink with the title's block shadow. Keyed on the tier as now.

ImportantEmil

The loader's cards breathe forever, over a bar that runs on a timer

WhatAfter the deal, all four cards loop loader-breathe (1.9s, infinite). The bar creeps to 90% on elapsed time against MIN_MS, not on anything actually loading.

Why it mattersA breathing loop is the pulsing-indicator pattern. It says "busy" without saying what is happening, and over a time-based bar it is decoration over a placeholder.

Recommended motionOne gesture with a meaning: a single riffle of the deck while something is outstanding, then stillness. If the load is slow, the riffle repeats every few seconds rather than breathing continuously. Fold it into finding 2's new loader rather than patching the old one.

Jakub Krehel — Production Polish

A casino game is a production surface seen in long repeated sessions: almost every finding is a transition that is half-built (a cut, a repaint, a mistimed leg) rather than one that should not exist.

  • Emil, stricterCut the loader's loop and the takeover's two ambient loops outright; keep the deal inside card 1's 950ms hold-back and let turbo cut it.
  • Jakub, stricterGive every screen hand-off an exit subtler than its entrance; animate the panels' origin from their controls.
  • Jhey, stricterTreat the loader, the hand-offs and the burst as set pieces: one continuous deal from the first frame to the board.
